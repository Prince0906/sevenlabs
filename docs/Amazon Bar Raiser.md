# Amazon Bar Raiser

The real program Aloud's [[Bar-Raiser Panel]] models.

## What it is
A Bar Raiser is *"an objective third-party adviser during the interview process"* (Amazon) — an interviewer from **outside the hiring team** who safeguards the hiring standard. Independence is structural: they're not on the team, often not even the same org, and **cannot** be Bar Raiser for hires in their own org. Amazon says there are **3,600+** of them; it's a volunteer role on top of the day job, with **3–12 months** of training (shadowing veteran Bar Raisers) centered on the [[Leadership Principles]].

## "Raise the bar"
Each hire must be **better than ~50% of current employees in that role** *and* have growth potential — tied to LP #6, *Hire and Develop the Best*. Philosophy: **"you'd rather hire no one than hire someone who wasn't excellent."**

## Veto authority (the nuance Aloud encodes)
- The Bar Raiser holds **veto power** — a candidate generally can't be hired without their agreement.
- It's **asymmetric**: the Bar Raiser can **block** even when everyone else wants to hire, but **cannot force** a hire the hiring manager opposes (both hold a no-hire veto).
- In practice the explicit veto is a rarely-used "nuclear option"; a good Bar Raiser uses the **Socratic method** in the **debrief** (which they chair) to drive a data-driven consensus.

Aloud mirrors this: the [[Judgment Pipeline]] computes a **deterministic** veto (not model-decided) only at follow-up depth `>= 2`, and the committee verdict is a separate judged call — see [[Bar-Raiser Panel]].

## STAR
Amazon explicitly recommends **STAR** (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for behavioral answers, with **specific metrics** and **"I" statements**. STAR answers are the evidence interviewers map back to specific LPs.
