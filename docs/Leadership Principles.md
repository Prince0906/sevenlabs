# Leadership Principles

Amazon publicly lists **16** Leadership Principles (expanded from 14 in 2021 with #15 and #16). They are the rubric the [[Bar-Raiser Panel]] scores against — and they're **public**, which is *why* they're not the [[Moat]] (the calibration is).

## The 16 (official)
1. **Customer Obsession** — start with the customer, work backwards.
2. **Ownership** — think long term; never sacrifice long-term value for short-term results.
3. **Invent and Simplify** — expect innovation; find ways to simplify.
4. **Are Right, A Lot** — strong judgment and good instincts.
5. **Learn and Be Curious** — never done learning.
6. **Hire and Develop the Best** — raise the bar with every hire (see [[Amazon Bar Raiser]]).
7. **Insist on the Highest Standards** — relentlessly high standards.
8. **Think Big** — bold direction that inspires results.
9. **Bias for Action** — speed matters; many decisions are reversible.
10. **Frugality** — accomplish more with less.
11. **Earn Trust** — listen, speak candidly, treat others respectfully.
12. **Dive Deep** — stay connected to the details; audit frequently.
13. **Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit** — challenge respectfully, then commit.
14. **Deliver Results** — focus on the key inputs; deliver with quality, on time.
15. **Strive to be Earth's Best Employer** *(2021)*.
16. **Success and Scale Bring Broad Responsibility** *(2021)*.

## How Aloud partitions them
Each [[Bar-Raiser Panel|seat]] is *given* a subset as its scorable rubric, so an evaluator literally never sees the others as scorable and can't launder a fluent answer:
- **Maya** (seat 0): Customer Obsession, Ownership, Invent & Simplify, Deliver Results, Hire & Develop.
- **Dev** (seat 1): Dive Deep, Highest Standards, Bias for Action, Frugality, Success & Scale.
- **Priya / Bar Raiser** (seat 2): Earn Trust, Have Backbone, Are Right A Lot, Think Big.

The partition is over the **rubric**, not the transcript — every seat scores the full candidate transcript, but only against its owned LPs ([[Judgment Pipeline]]).
