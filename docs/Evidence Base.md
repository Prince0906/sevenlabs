# Evidence Base

The product thesis ([[Vision]]) is a chain, and each link is evidence-backed.

## 1. Interview anxiety is near-universal and lowers ratings
- **92% of U.S. adults** are stressed by at least one thing about interviewing; being "too nervous" is the single biggest fear (Harris Interactive / Everest College, 2013). Nervousness hits lower-earners hardest (22% vs 11%).
- Meta-analysis: self-reported interview anxiety ↔ interview performance correlates **r = −.19** (Powell, Stanley & Brown, 2018). The penalty runs through *observable* anxious nonverbal behavior — the "qualified but nervous" candidate is systematically under-rated. That gap is exactly what a rehearsal tool targets.

## 2. Rehearsal reduces anxiety AND improves performance
- **Deliberate practice** (Ericsson): a defined task + immediate feedback + repetition + error-exploitation. The [[Bar-Raiser Panel]] + [[Judgment Pipeline|report]] is a deliberate-practice loop.
- Public-speaking-anxiety meta-analysis: **Hedges g = 0.74 post, 1.11 at follow-up** (30 RCTs; Ebrahimi et al., 2019) — and **no significant difference between in-person and technology-delivered** interventions (p = 0.814). Directly supports a software model.
- Simulated-interview RCT: practice with a virtual interviewer raised both skill and **self-confidence** (Humm et al., 2014). A stronger one (Smith et al., 2021) found trained participants hit **competitive employment at 25% vs 0% of controls (OR ≈ 16)**.

## 3. The mechanism: self-efficacy
Bandura's #1 source of self-efficacy is **mastery experience** — repeated *successful* rehearsal. Task-specific self-efficacy is associated with ~28% gains in work performance (Stajkovic & Luthans, 1998). This is why [[Confidence Metric]] is the north star.

## 4. Market (directional only)
Interview-prep tooling ~$500M (2025, ~15% CAGR); coding-interview platforms ~$450M–$3.35B depending on definition. Treat vendor reports as sizing, not proof — the RCT/meta-analytic sources above are the citable backbone.
